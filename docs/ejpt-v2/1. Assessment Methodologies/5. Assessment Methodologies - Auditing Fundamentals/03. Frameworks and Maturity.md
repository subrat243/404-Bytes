## Business Needs

Organizations require structured approaches to manage security, compliance, and risk. These needs drive the adoption of frameworks.

**Explanation**  
Businesses must protect data, ensure compliance, and manage risks efficiently. Without structure, security becomes inconsistent.

**Example**  
A company handling customer data implements security policies to avoid data breaches and legal penalties.

---

## Frameworks

Frameworks provide standardized guidelines and best practices for implementing security and governance.

**Explanation**  
They act as a blueprint to design, implement, and manage security controls.

**Example**  
Using a framework like ISO 27001, a company defines policies, controls, and procedures for securing information.

---

## Common Security Frameworks

As shown in the document (_page 5_), major frameworks include ISO/IEC 27000, COBIT, NIST, CIS, CMMC, and ASD.

---

## ISO/IEC 27000 Series

**Explanation**  
A globally recognized standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). It is broad and applies to all organizations.

**Key Parts**

- ISO 27001 → Requirements for ISMS
    
- ISO 27002 → Security controls and best practices
    

**Example**  
A bank uses ISO 27001 to implement access control, encryption, and risk management policies.

---

## COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies)

**Explanation**  
A framework for IT governance and management, focusing on aligning IT with business goals.

**Example**  
An enterprise uses COBIT to ensure IT investments support business strategy and reduce operational risks.

---

## NIST SP 800-53

**Explanation**  
A catalog of security and privacy controls used mainly by U.S. federal systems.

**Example**  
A government agency applies NIST controls like access control, incident response, and auditing.

---

## CIS Controls

**Explanation**  
A set of 18 prioritized security controls designed to prevent common cyber-attacks.

**Example**  
Implementing CIS Control 1 (Inventory of Assets) helps track all devices in a network to prevent unauthorized access.

---

## CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification)

**Explanation**  
A certification model used in the U.S. Defense Industrial Base to assess cybersecurity maturity across 5 levels.

**Levels Overview (from diagram on page 12)**

- Level 1 → Basic hygiene
    
- Level 2 → Documented
    
- Level 3 → Managed
    
- Level 4 → Reviewed
    
- Level 5 → Optimizing
    

**Example**  
A defense contractor must achieve a required CMMC level to work with the Department of Defense.

---

## ASD Essential Eight

**Explanation**  
A security framework developed by the Australian Cyber Security Centre focusing on 8 critical controls.

**Controls (from page 14 image)**

- Application control
    
- Patch applications
    
- Configure macros
    
- User application hardening
    
- Restrict admin privileges
    
- Patch OS
    
- Multi-factor authentication
    
- Daily backups
    

**Example**  
A company enables MFA and regular patching to reduce ransomware attacks.

---

## Maturity Models

Frameworks often include maturity levels to measure how well security practices are implemented.

**Explanation**  
Maturity models help organizations understand their current security posture and improve over time.

**Example**  
An organization at Level 1 may have basic controls, while Level 5 continuously improves and optimizes security.

---

## Why Frameworks Matter

**Explanation**  
Frameworks provide structure, consistency, and measurable security improvements.

**Example**  
Without a framework → random security practices  
With a framework → standardized, auditable, and scalable security

---

## Implementation Concept

As indicated in the final slide (_page 17: "How to implement?"_), implementation involves adopting a framework and aligning it with business needs.

**Basic Steps**

- Identify business requirements
    
- Choose appropriate framework
    
- Implement controls
    
- Monitor and audit
    
- Improve maturity level
    

**Example**  
A startup adopts CIS Controls first, then gradually moves toward ISO 27001 certification as it grows.
